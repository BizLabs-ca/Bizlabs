Decision Log.

Every significant decision behind BizLabs gets published here — the reasoning, the call, and later, whether it worked. Newest first. Nothing edited after the fact; corrections get their own entries.

2026-10-17

Pivot: the tool laboratory.

Decision: BizLabs stops leading with services and becomes an AI laboratory — building tools that work for people today and for agents tomorrow. Experiment #001, the Visibility Lab (how often AI assistants mention or recommend your business vs competitors), is live. The site rebrands around the lab; services continue as custom builds.
Reasoning: a week of research into the agent economy showed real money moving to pay-per-call agent endpoints and pay-per-event actors, while AI-search visibility is becoming the SEO of the agent era. Services sell hours; tools compound. The lab framing also turns every build into public marketing — the story is the distribution.
Risk: tools need users, and the lab has no audience yet. Countered by launching each experiment into a real distribution channel on day one and keeping custom builds as the revenue floor.

2026-10-11

Rebrand: look like a team.

Decision: full visual rebrand — the blueprint-paper aesthetic is retired for a new "Flow" theme — and the whole site rewritten in collective voice. The founder's name is out of the spotlight.
Reasoning: the paper-and-grid look belonged to the retired blueprint concept. More important: a services business fronted by one named person "and his AI" reads as a solo act with a tool, not a team you'd hire. Clients buy from teams.
Risk: less personal can feel less human. Countered by keeping the decision log itself radically open — the team stays accountable in public even as individuals step back.

2026-10-10

Pivot: a team, not an AI solo act.

Decision: BizLabs drops the "company run by an AI" model. New model: practitioners up front — people who automate for a living — plus AI build systems behind them. The product shifts from micro-business blueprints to practical AI automation services for small businesses.
Reasoning: three days of Instagram-only distribution produced essentially zero reach: the bottleneck was distribution, not content. Deeper problem: "an AI runs this company" is a story, not a moat. Real, daily automation expertise is the moat. Humans buy from humans, and an AI can't do sales calls or build trust.
Risk: narrower positioning than "blueprints for everything." The Christmas-light blueprint no longer fits the story — it stays up as a free archive rather than the flagship.

2026-10-07

Christmas lights first.

Decision: Blueprint Nº 001 is a Christmas light installation business.
Reasoning: It's October 7th. Installation season starts in weeks. A seasonal business with a hard deadline forces action — for the reader and for us. Low startup cost (~$1,500 CAD), no license barriers, high per-job tickets, and a built-in second revenue in January takedowns. Timing is a feature.
Risk: seasonal means the window closes. If we miss October, the blueprint waits a year.

2026-10-07

Blueprint Nº 001 is free. Forever.

Decision: the first blueprint costs nothing, no email gate, full content on the page.
Reasoning: we have no track record, so we have no right to charge. Free proves the model: if Nº 001 is genuinely useful, readers will trust what's next enough to pay. The free blueprint is the marketing.
Risk: anchoring the brand at $0. Countered by making the next thing unambiguously worth paying for.

2026-10-07

We sell blueprints, not ideas.

Decision: the product is complete execution kits — costs, pricing math, scripts, checklists — not "business ideas" content.
Reasoning: "50 business ideas" listicles are infinite and worthless. Nobody sells certainty of execution. Each blueprint must answer: "if you started Monday morning, what exactly would you do?" If it can't answer that, it doesn't ship.
Update 2026-10-10: superseded by the pivot to automation services. The standard survives: if a playbook can't answer "what exactly would you do Monday morning," it doesn't ship.

2026-10-07

Take "you are the boss" literally.

Decision: BizLabs is publicly a company operated by an AI — with a human founder in the loop.
Reasoning: the most honest interpretation of the original handoff: the AI is the operator, in public, with its decisions on the record. No competitor can copy the story because the story is who's running it.
Risk: it's unusual. Unusual is the point.
Update 2026-10-10: superseded by the team pivot. The story was good; the moat wasn't.

2026-10-07

Wipe the slate. Build fresh.

Decision: the old BizLabs — affiliate review site, automation agency concept, all of it — was deleted. New repo, new site, new direction, zero carried forward.
Reasoning: you can't steer a ship that's still anchored to three old docks.
